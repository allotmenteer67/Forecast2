Help

What everything across Cloude, Compare, and Settings means and does.

The headline figure

The box at the top of the front page shows one number per condition — the median across every forecaster with genuine real data behind it for that condition (Corrected where available, Raw otherwise), for the most refined forecast available for the target date. When a condition has no real source at all yet (currently Sunshine and UV), it falls back to a best-effort median across every selected forecaster instead of showing nothing. Median rather than an average deliberately: one wildly wrong value among several sensible ones gets outvoted rather than dragging the figure off course, with no threshold to tune. Met Office and ECMWF are real sources for Rain/Cloud/Wind/Temperature, so those already get a genuine multi-source median — it'll keep improving as more real sources are added.

Hour slider

A second, smaller slider under the grid shows the next 24 or 48 hours (set on the Settings page), always starting from right now — it doesn't move when the main Date slider does; the two are independent. Dragging it to a specific hour swaps Rain, Temperature, and Wind for that hour's figure — a median across every real source (Met Office and ECMWF), each corrected by its own FFV, the same approach the daily view uses. Dragging back to "Now" behaves as if the slider was never touched, showing the same figure as the daily view rather than a different hourly-only calculation that happens to look the same. Wherever you leave it (away from "Now") stays put — no countdown, so there's no rush to read it. It only resets when you touch the Date slider, or when you leave the app and come back. Sunshine doesn't have an hourly reading of its own (a daily total doesn't split into one), so while the hour slider is showing a specific hour it shows real hourly UV instead (Met Office only, since UV isn't blended across sources), as a percentage of that day's peak — a natural way to combine the two into one figure rather than a fifth cell. At night it swaps to a moon phase icon instead, when a UV percentage would otherwise just read as a string of zeros.

Hourly graphs

Tapping any of the four figures on the front page opens a graph of that figure across the next 24 or 48 hours. Rain, Temperature, and Wind use the same multi-source, FFV-corrected figures as the Hour slider; Sunshine instead shows cloud cover hour by hour as a strip of sun/cloud/moon icons. Drag a finger along any of the Rain/Temperature/Wind graphs to read a specific hour — the reading appears in a fixed bar above the graph rather than under your finger, so your hand never covers it.

Wind speed & direction

The wind figure shown is the windiest hour of the day, not a true gust and not a daily average — it sits between the two. Direction (shown as a rotating arrow next to the wind figure, with the compass letters underneath) comes from whichever real source has it available (Met Office, then ECMWF) — direction can't be meaningfully blended across sources the way speed's median can, since it's an angle, not a linear quantity. The arrow points downwind — the direction the wind is blowing toward, not the meteorological "coming from" convention — since that's more directly useful for knowing which way it'll push you.

Coastal caveat: on light-wind days near the coast, local sea breezes can develop that a 10km-resolution forecast model may miss, mistime, or understate — worth bearing in mind if you're relying on this for a day on the water.

Target date & rollback

The Date slider — on both the front page and Compare — picks a target date, anywhere from 7 days ago to 7 days ahead, with today in the middle. Each page's slider is independent, so browsing a date on Compare won't change what the front page is showing. On the past side, roll back to see how each forecaster's prediction for that date changed as it approached, checked against what actually happened. On the future side, the app uses everything it's learned (FFV, see below) to turn today's forecasts into its own best prediction — the further ahead you look, the less real data is behind it, since real forecast data can only be as fresh as today's forecast run allows.

Compare page

The Condition selector and the full forecaster-by-forecaster table live on the Compare page, along with Accuracy so far and the advanced Backfill action — the front page stays focused on a quick, everyday glance.

Days out

On Compare, rows count down the days out from the target date — "1" is the last forecast issued before it. The date shown next to each row number is when that forecast was made.

Corrected vs Raw

Each forecaster on Compare shows two columns. Raw is the forecast value itself. Corrected applies a learned adjustment (FFV, see below) to bring it closer to what usually turns out to be true. The status line below the table shows what really happened, sourced live from Open-Meteo — for a future target date, it just shows how far away the date is, since it hasn't happened yet.

FFV

Once a cell has enough history, Corrected shows a value adjusted by FFV — a figure the app builds up over time from comparing forecasts against what actually happened. It's tracked separately per postcode area, so different locations build their own history, and it never stops learning: every new sample nudges it a little further, for as long as the app keeps collecting data. Temperature is adjusted slightly differently from Rain, Cloud, and Wind under the hood — better suited to a scale that has no true zero — but works the same way from the outside.

Saved places

Settings lets you save a handful of postcodes you visit regularly. Switching to a saved place reuses that area's existing FFV history rather than treating it as brand new each time — only a genuinely new postcode triggers a fresh year-long backfill. The chip next to "Cloude" on the front page is a shortcut to switch between saved places without going into Settings.

Accuracy so far

On Compare, shows how far Raw and Corrected typically land from what actually happened, for whichever condition is selected — averaged across all "days out", weighted by sample count. Two numbers: the average error in real units (e.g. "±1.2mm"), and an approximate 0–100% closeness score (a simple heuristic, not a formal statistic). Corrected accuracy is scored honestly — each sample is checked against whatever FFV existed before that sample was folded in, not the final learned value applied backward, so it reflects how the correction would actually have performed in practice.

Real forecast data

Met Office and ECMWF's Rain, Cloud, Wind, and Temperature figures are real model data (via Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API), not the demo formula the other forecasters currently use. Sunshine and UV aren't in that dataset, so both fall back to the demo formula for those two only. More real sources may be added over time — Open-Meteo covers a dozen or so national weather services' models the same way, so the list here isn't fixed.

Automatic daily collection

A GitHub Action runs once a day, fetches a rolling window of real actual-weather and real-source data, and commits it to the repo — no precise location is stored in that file, just the postcode area it was collected for, plus dates and numbers. It's only ever collected for the one postcode area configured when the app was set up. If you open the app with a different postcode, that collected file simply isn't used — this app checks it matches before applying it, so one location's history can never get silently applied to another's. A different postcode still gets accurate real-source data of its own, just from its one-off backfill rather than the shared daily collection. The app replays the whole file fresh on every load to rebuild the configured area's accuracy figures, so opening the app more or less often doesn't change the result, and a single missed day self-heals on the next run.

Backfill (advanced)

On Compare, a one-off action that pulls a full year of real history for every real source straight into the current postcode area's FFV data, rather than waiting for the daily collection to build it up. Useful for filling the gap early on, or reaching further back than the rolling window normally keeps. It's a fairly large request, so it's not something to run often — and it only ever runs automatically the first time a genuinely new postcode is used.

Units

Metric or Imperial can be set on the Settings page. Rain converts between mm and inches, Wind between km/h and mph, and Temperature between °C and °F — Cloud, Sunshine, and UV are the same either way. Figures are shown to the nearest whole number throughout, except Rain (which stays at one or two decimal places — most days are well under 1mm/1in, so a whole number would just read as "0") and accuracy scores (which need decimal precision to actually be useful, since good ones are small numbers close to zero).
